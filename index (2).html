“在追求卓越的路上，我始终在学习如何平衡选择与坚持。”

回顾本科生涯，我的学术路径并非坦途，却因“不走捷径”而愈发厚重。在专业分流的关键时刻，我选择了挑战自我，投身于竞争极度激烈的数学与应用数学（概率统计方向）。尽管当时我的 GPA 在信息与计算科学（计算数学方向）同级七十余名同学中高居 第 2 位，但我仍渴望在随机分析与概率的海洋中磨砺思维。

这种纯粹的初心虽让我在随后的排名竞争与荣誉评定中经历了些许“戏剧性”的波折，但也正是这段跨方向的深度修读，极大地拓宽了我的数学视野，赋予了我从随机性视角审视确定性问题的独特能力。

推免阶段，我选择回归初心，深耕计算数学领域。虽曾因早期的专业跨度在保研过程中历经波折，但这段“跨界”的磨砺最终内化为我研究最优化算法时的独特底蕴——概率的灵动与计算的严谨在此交汇。

“造化弄人”终是序曲。选择的意义不在于结果是否全然完美，而在于这一路累积的厚度，以及在波折中愈发坚定的科研志向。

⚙️ Competitions and interdisciplinary explorations

Electric Field Lines and Equipotential Surface Visualizer: A Highly Interactive Physics Teaching Tool Based on Web Technologies 2024.12 – 2025.10
  • Project type: Entry for the First USTC Electromagnetism Animation Design Competition
  • Role: Co-author (with Zhong Chengyi; mainly responsible for theoretical derivation and algorithm design)
  • Core contribution: To address difficult issues such as singular curves around saddle points and the visual imbalance caused by highly uneven charges, I introduced adaptive step-size control and a dynamic density-reduction mechanism. These substantially improved the smoothness and visual balance of the field lines.
  • Progress: The work has clear interactivity and teaching value. The competition has ended, and we are still improving the outcome with the expectation of applying for related intellectual property later.
Solutions and Discussions on Electric Field-Line Equations for Several Simple Electrostatic Models 2023.09 – 2024.01
  • Project type: Entry for the USTC Electromagnetism Mini-Paper Competition, Fall 2023 (course instructor: Sun Xia)
  • Role: Co-author (with Lin Qihao; mainly responsible for the core theoretical derivations)
  • Core contribution: Using elementary integration methods from ODEs and other basic mathematical tools, I derived analytic expressions for field-line equations in several simple electrostatic models, including equal like charges, equal opposite charges, and unequal charges. Through a close interaction between mathematics and physics, we discussed the distribution of field lines in dipole-type models and some more general regularities.
  • Progress: The derivations were rigorous and physically suggestive. The project entered the school-level defense and eventually won the Third Prize.
The Problem of Effective Friend-Making in Social Networks (University-stage development) 2022.09 – 2023.06
  • Project type: Research topic for the USTC seminar course “Science and Society” (course instructor: Liu Yongqiang)
  • Role: Core team member (responsible for writing, algorithm derivation, and presentation)
  • Core contribution: We studied a graph-theoretic model of social networking oriented toward maximizing the acceptance probability of a target user. Building on my earlier secondary-school work, I further explored influence maximization, introduced the idea of community structure to classify isolated nodes, and compared exhaustive, greedy, and recursive algorithms. Real Facebook network data were used to validate the recursive algorithm on large-scale instances.
  • Progress: The presentation was detailed, logically organized, and well-argued, and the course was graded A+.
An Analysis of Conformity in Online Environments 2022.09 – 2023.01
  • Project type: Independent research paper for the USTC course “Psychology for University Students” (course instructor: Wang Jue)
  • Role: Sole author
  • Core contribution: I independently wrote a research paper using the dual-process model of conformity to analyze typical online collective phenomena that emerged during campus lockdown, such as “dorm puppies,” “campus crawling,” and disruptive behavior in online classes. I also drew on state-anxiety theory to discuss how emotional release may trigger irrational conformity online.
  • Progress: The paper was judged to be logically sound and well argued, and the course was graded A+.
